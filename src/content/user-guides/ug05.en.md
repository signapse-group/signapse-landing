## Goal

Find and open an article or event, read its evidence, and compare the source article. Recognize what you could check, what is interpretation, and what remains missing or contradictory.

The main reading flow is **Event → evidence → source article**. You can also start with an article to check that source's content directly.

## Before you start

Sign in to Signapse and choose the event or article you want to read. Event and article access are granted separately. When you can read the article and the evidence links to it, choose **View article** to read it in Signapse.

Check the workspace and displayed scope when starting from the [Overview](/en/docs/ug04). On event or article lists, read the active search terms and filters; do not assume all results have been filtered by your watchlist.

## Steps

### 1. Find and open content

Open **Events** in the app navigation. Enter keywords in the search box to search event titles or descriptions. Wait for results to load, check the sort order, and change pages if needed. Select the appropriate event title or its detail button.

To find an article directly, open **News articles**, enter words from its title, check the source and publication time in the results, and select the title to open it. Searching article titles does not search the entire article content.

From the Overview, select an event or article title to open its detail panel. Use the full-page action when you need more detail. **Main evidence** in the event quick detail panel shows only part of the list; read **Evidence** on the full event page for the remaining items when available.

### 2. Read the event before interpreting it

Read the title, description, status, and **Occurred at** time. Check **Related assets** and themes when available to determine whether the content relates to what you are following.

When asset roles are provided, distinguish the event's **primary subject**, an **affected asset**, and a **reference asset**. Mentioning an asset does not establish a price impact. For themes, read primary or secondary roles when available; asset roles and the evidence roles below describe different relationships.

Distinguish the event occurrence time, the source article's publication time, and the content update time in Signapse.

If you read **Market reactions**, check the asset, direction, time horizon, and accompanying reasoning when available. Distinguish inferred impact from observed price movement; see [Reading market charts](/en/docs/ug06) to compare price data.

### 3. Read each evidence item

Under **Evidence**, check the article title, news outlet, publication time, note, and confidence when available. Read the displayed role alongside the content rather than relying only on article counts:

| Role | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| **Primary** | What does the article used as the event's main evidence actually state? |
| **Supporting** | Which event details does the article supplement or support? |
| **Update** | What information has been added, and does its timing differ from earlier articles? |
| **Contradicting** | Which details disagree with the event or another source? |

Role labels and **Confidence** scores help you read how the system organizes information; they do not replace checking the source article. Do not interpret confidence as the probability of a price rise or fall. Several articles repeating one source do not necessarily provide independent confirmations.

### 4. Open and compare the source article

From an evidence item, select **View article** when available to read it in Signapse. Check the source name, publication time, description, and detailed content. A title or short description alone does not mean you have read the full article.

Select **Open original link** on evidence in the full event page or on the article to open the external source in a new tab. In the event quick detail panel, this action is labeled **Open original**. The link appears only when the data includes a source article address.

Check that it is the intended article, then compare the asset or subject, timing, and content supporting the event description. Distinguish what the source actually states from Signapse's interpretation. Return to the event to read other evidence items when needed.

### 5. Note missing or contradictory information

If a source cannot be opened, content is incomplete, or the event has no evidence, note what you could not check. Do not fill gaps with assumptions.

If sources disagree, compare publication times, scope, and the specific conflicting details. An update may concern a different time; titles alone are insufficient to establish that two articles contradict each other. If the difference remains unresolved, keep the conclusion uncertain rather than choosing a source solely because its confidence score is higher.

## Expected result

You can trace an event to evidence and its source article, and identify what remains unverified.

## Troubleshooting

- **No search results:** try shorter search terms, check the sort order and current filters. No results do not establish that no relevant market event exists.
- **No evidence or article content:** note the current data state; see [Missing data or features](/en/docs/ug08). Do not treat an event description as a substitute for the source article.
- **An article will not open or its link is missing:** A link may be absent because no source address is available. Read the on-screen message and see [Missing data or features](/en/docs/ug08).
- **Loading fails, a source is inaccessible, or a contradiction remains unresolved:** note the event or article, source, any message, time checked, and details needing clarification. See [Missing data or features](/en/docs/ug08) for help.
